Concept · Original Prototype
✓ Complete
Where it all started. Built a military compound blockout in Unreal Engine 5 to prove the concept actually works. Integrated the ALS (Advanced Locomotion System) for third-person character control. Built a prototype dollhouse camera to test the core vision: design underground from a side-view ant farm cutaway, then drop into your creation in full 3D and walk the corridors you built. The question was simple: is it fun, does it look good? It did. That was the moment AIRLOCK stopped being an idea and started being a game.

Foundation · Documentation & Lore
✓ Complete
The world built before the game. Ashgate as the ruined city. The Altered (Feral, Familiars, Stalkers, Legion) as four-tier enemies with their own lore. The Settlers — 14 distinct classes from Soldier to Cook to Engineer to Elder, each with signature modules. The post-war UGA (United Global Alliance) as the lost world's last unity. The Sonic War 300 years ago. The Legion reveal arc. Multi-airlock metro network. Confluence wiki built out, design docs locked, dos and don'ts written down. Every system built since references this foundation.
Foundation · Audio Direction
✓ Complete
Audio reference locked: The Last of Us / TLOU Part II for emotional weight, Days Gone for survival texture. Hans Zimmer for the orchestral spine. "Calm Before The Storm" written and scored as the trailer theme. Diegetic SFX rules: no paper rustles, no terminal beeps, no clockwork mechanical metaphors. Submix routing locked (Master → LowHealth submix → low-pass filter). The sonic identity of AIRLOCK locked before any major audio shipped.
Foundation · UI Art Direction
✓ Complete
After exploring over 20 HUD variations across 10 distinct style directions, the painted concept art aesthetic was locked in. Ink and paper palette. Cormorant Garamond display, Permanent Marker labels, EB Garamond italic body. Brushstroke buttons. Ink-wash halo backings instead of hard borders. Cream on near-black. Every UI screen from HUD to main menu to inventory carries the same DNA. The visual identity of AIRLOCK, crystallised before code touched it.
Foundation · Painted Icon Library
✓ Complete
91 painted monochrome icons delivered, covering survival bars, status effects, weapons, consumables, loot, HUD chrome, banners, structural frames, screen overlays, map pins, Settler activities, and skill tree parents. Cream sumi-ink brushstrokes on black, visible paint strokes, no vector feel. A full sprite sheet, ready to drop straight into Unreal. Every icon you see in-game came from this library.

Pre-Production · Grid System
✓ Complete
The backbone of the entire game. Built the GridManager system in C++ from scratch — world-to-grid coordinate conversion, cell states, multi-floor support for stacking levels underground. Powers both the dollhouse / ant-farm side-view and the in-engine 3D base. Modules snap to the grid with adjacency detection — walls and doors auto-generate between rooms. This is the system everything else is built on top of.
Pre-Production · Character
✓ Complete
Took the ALS system apart and rebuilt it for AIRLOCK. Customised movement feel, camera behaviour, transitions between underground and surface modes. The character controller handles tight corridors and wide-open surface exploration seamlessly — two very different feels, one character.
Pre-Production · UI System
✓ Complete
Built the full main menu system from scratch — epilepsy warning, brightness calibration, press-any-key splash, main menu with all sub-screens and settings. Custom-designed with a worn aesthetic that feels like it belongs in a bunker.
Pre-Production · HUD System
✓ Complete
In-game HUD: health, stamina, compass, status effects, notifications. Full inventory system — equip weapons, fire, swap, drop, pick up. Primary and secondary weapon slots. Items degrade over time. Nothing lasts forever in AIRLOCK.
Pre-Production · Combat
✓ Complete
Weapons you can equip, fire, and feel. Ranged and melee integrated with the inventory system — every weapon degrades with use and can break. Ammunition is scarce. Combat is designed to feel desperate, not heroic. You're not a soldier — you're a survivor.
Pre-Production · Module System
✓ Complete
The module system fully integrated into the HUD — browse 14+ categories, see every detail before you place (tier, size, morale bonus, build time, power draw, water usage). Place, move, remove. Started as a Blueprint system, transitioned mid-development to Level Instance modules to speed up authoring. The architecture decision that unlocked authoring speed.
Pre-Production · Horde System
✓ Complete
An optimised horde system — large numbers of The Altered on screen without tanking performance. Spawn, detection, navigation, swarming attacks. AI states: idle wanderers, alerted searchers, full-speed chasers, swarming attackers. They hunt. They swarm. They overwhelm. Daytime is tense. Night is deadly.
Pre-Production · Loot
✓ Complete
Lootable items scattered throughout ruined buildings, abandoned vehicles, and the remains of those who didn't make it. Food, weapons, medicine, blueprints, raw materials, rare components. Every surface trip is a risk-reward calculation — push deeper for better loot, or play it safe near the airlock?
Pre-Production · MetaHuman
✓ Complete
Out with the placeholder, in with the real thing. The ALS mannequin replaced with a fully assembled MetaHuman character — realistic face, hair, skin, expressions. The foundation for cutscenes, emotional storytelling, and characters you actually care about. First MetaHuman head swapped in for the first story-locked NPC.
Pre-Production · Marv (Companion)
✓ Complete
You shouldn't face the surface alone. Marv follows you — engaging enemies, sniffing out hidden loot, reacting to danger before you see it. Sit system, full locomotion, pose rules so he never floats or moves while resting. He has personality. He has moods. He remembers.
Pre-Production · Tier System
✓ Complete
Every module can be upgraded through five visual and functional tiers: Cave, Structured Cave, Brick + Wood, Concrete + Tile, Metal Sci-fi. Each upgrade costs resources, consumes utilities, and delivers real improvements. Mesh swaps, upgrade and downgrade flow, in-engine and working. Tier 0 is a shared cave that every module starts from. Tier 4 is the endgame "you rebuilt civilisation" payoff.
Pre-Production · Mining & Interact
✓ Complete
A universal Press-to-Interact framework drives every interaction in the game: doors, loot, NPCs, ore nodes, terminals. Platform-aware icons swap between keyboard, Xbox, and PlayStation glyphs automatically. The mining loop integrates with grid depth — the deeper you dig, the rarer the ores you find. Copper, iron, silver, gold and beyond.
Pre-Production · Skill System
✓ Complete
A shared skill tree between player and Settlers. 17 parent categories, over 130 sub-skills, eight tiers each with ten sub-levels for a full 80-level ladder per skill. Pistols, cooking, brewing, husbandry, alchemy, fishing, tailoring, medical, and more. Learn by doing, read books, be taught by Settlers, or spend research points. Settlers level faster while stationed at their matching module.

Vertical Slice · NPC Foundation
✓ Complete
The C++ backbone for every NPC in AIRLOCK. A 9-state behaviour machine (Idle, Follow, Independent, Tasked, Combat, Flee, Injured, Conversational, Dead). A 7-faction team system (Player, Airlock, Altered, Legion, Raider, Wildlife, Neutral). Component stack for brains, combat, injury, tasking, dialogue. Every future Settler, enemy, and wildlife NPC slots in cleanly on top of this. Zombies now see multiple targets, not just the player.
Vertical Slice · Smart NPCs
✓ Complete
The first proof that the airlock has people in it. Voice pipeline with TTS-generated lines and inline emotion tags. Per-NPC Data Tables drive line lookup by category (Greeting, IdleBark, WorkBark, Complaint, Bump, Story). Painted Subtitle Widget with stack mode for overlapping voices, proximity gating, italic-paren convention for non-speech. Activity Anchors so NPCs naturally migrate between content (chairs, workbenches, planning boards). Bump Component for visceral knockback. Power-aware barks fire instantly when the network state changes. The codebase is ready for every Settler class to slot in.
Vertical Slice · Painted HUD
✓ Complete
The painted concept art direction landed in engine. Every HUD element rebuilt around the locked painted-monochrome aesthetic: cream sumi-ink on near-black, Cormorant Garamond display, Permanent Marker chrome labels. Ink-wash backings replaced hard borders. Compass strip, day-and-time stack, painted survival dial, weapon cards with brushstroke dividers, painted interact prompts, full subtitle widget with stack mode. Module padlock states for class-locked rooms. Auto-flashlight that engages when the airlock loses power or night falls.
Vertical Slice · Survival Stats
✓ Complete
The body keeps score. Stamina drains as you sprint, climb, fight, then recovers in safety. Hunger drains over a 28 in-game day curve — grounded in real human physiology, you can survive weeks without food but being underfed makes everything harder. Thirst drains over 48 in-game hours — fast, urgent, you can't ignore it. Low hunger doesn't instakill, it accelerates stamina drain. Radiation as a system is wired but parked for V2. The vitals dial in the bottom-left of the HUD updates live.
Vertical Slice · Day & Time Cycle
✓ Complete
A fully dynamic day-night cycle. 45 real-world minutes equals one in-game day. The time-of-day clock ticks over in the top-right of the HUD. Every system that cares about time reads from the same source of truth (Ultra Dynamic Sky): construction timers, Settler schedules, skill XP rates, expedition fatigue, weather, lighting. Toxic night-time air mechanic queued. Daytime is tense. Night is deadly.
Vertical Slice · Power & Electricity
✓ Complete
The base now has a real heartbeat. Generators with green / amber / red status LEDs, audio start-loop-stop with fail-attempt boot variants, vibration that rattles the housing while running, clean interact toggle. Multi-generator networks aggregate capacity. Sustained overload trips the breaker after five seconds. Powered lights and ambient audio emitters subscribe to the network and react instantly. Auto-flashlight engages when night falls or the airlock goes dark.
Vertical Slice · Dialogue & Voice
✓ Complete
The dialogue layer is live. Subtitle widget pushes painted-monochrome speech below the HUD with stack mode for overlapping voices. Voice lines drive through per-NPC Data Tables by category. The first NPC talks, the player hears her, the painted subtitle reads underneath. Generators hum, NPCs talk, ambient emitters push their own subtitles ("*Static*", "*Generator hum*"). The emotional layer of the game has landed in UI.
Vertical Slice · City & Foliage Tools
✓ Complete
A full toolkit for authoring Ashgate at speed without losing artistic control. Procedural city tool traces streets and arrays building blocks. City Block Breaker editor utility takes any block and explodes it into individually editable static meshes for hand-art-direction. Foliage Cascade marketplace-ready PCG node spawns hierarchical biomes of trees, juveniles, saplings, ground cover, scrub across landscape AND static meshes. PCG_Include / PCG_Remove tags. Bake to InstancedFoliageActor for editable Foliage Painter integration. The whole pipeline ships marketplace-ready.

Operations Room with the Planning Board and salvaged radio desk. Workhouse with the multi-discipline crafting stations. Bunkroom with the bunk migration mechanic. Vertical Slice · Module Unlocks
→ Next
Settlers unlock modules. No Engineer in the base? No Tier 2+ upgrades, full stop. No Chemist? No explosives, no fuel, no pharmaceuticals. No Gunsmith? No silencer modding. Your rescue roster literally determines what your base can become. Kingdom-Tale-style progression. Rescue drives unlocks. The survival loop gains meaning. The "every Settler matters" beat.
Vertical Slice · Construction State
→ Next
Module upgrades stop being instant. When you kick off an upgrade, the room enters an Under Construction state. Time passes. Scaffolding appears. If the upgrade digs deeper, ore nodes spawn in the newly exposed rock — mine them before the timer ends or lose them forever. Settlers physically shove Kore out of the corridor during construction. The act of building feels real.

Watch them become Settlers. Venture back for supplies. Survive the night. The demo ends with a hook that makes you need to know what happens next. This goes onto Steam for the world to play.

Release · 1.0 Launch
▶ The Vision Realised
All systems. Full narrative. Complete economy. The game Henry has carried in his head for over a decade, fully realised. A survival base-builder where you don't just survive — you rebuild. You trade. You explore. You discover the truth about what happened 300 years ago. Your way, in your airlock, in your corner of a broken world.
Release · Console Builds
→ Post-Launch
PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and Switch 2 builds. UE5 makes the actual ports feasible. Each platform requires certification: TRC for PlayStation, XR for Xbox, NCT for Nintendo. Cert is a real time sink — months not weeks. Console launches typically follow the PC launch by 3-6 months but expand the audience massively. The reach moment.
Release · Localization
→ Post-Launch
French, German, Spanish, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Polish, Simplified Chinese, Japanese, Korean. UI strings extracted to localizable tables. Subtitles for every voice line. Re-recording per language is out of scope for V1 — subtitle-only is the indie standard. The Steam wishlist conversion rate in non-English markets lives or dies on this.
Release · Trading & Economy
→ Post-Launch
Set up automated trade routes between your airlocks. Previous airlocks you've moved on from don't sit empty, they become trading partners. Barter, buy, sell with NPC vendors. Supply and demand shifts based on scarcity. Surplus food but need medicine? Another airlock has the opposite problem. Trade routes emerge. An underground economy grows from the ashes, using UGA Credits as the post-war currency.
Release · Multi-Airlock System
→ Post-Launch
One airlock isn't enough. Discover new underground locations: pre-war military bunkers, research annexes, mine shafts, civic shelters. Each has unique starting features and unique upgrades. Build, max out, leave, find another. Your previous airlocks stay intact and active. Specialise them: your farm, your armoury, your research hub. Rebuild civilisation, one airlock at a time.
Release · Underground Metro
→ Post-Launch
Every airlock has a buried metro station at its deepest point. Excavate down through the grid. Clear the rubble. Assign an Engineer Settler to restore power. Reconnect the line to the wider UGA rail network. Once active, fast-travel between your airlocks without touching Ashgate. Supplies move. Settlers transfer where needed. You ride the rails through dark tunnels beneath a dead world. The spine of your underground civilisation.
Release · Global Maps
→ Post-Launch
Ashgate is just one city. Beyond the UK launch map: Tokyo's metro tunnels, New York's subway network, Berlin's bunkers, Moscow's deepest-in-the-world metro, the Australian outback's mining shafts, the Brazilian favela's vertical stack, the Amazon's jungle-swallowed cities. Each map regional-flavoured: unique architecture, unique threats, unique resources, unique survivor stories. Every map is its own self-contained adventure that connects to the larger network via the Underground Metro.
Release · Multiplayer
→ Long-Term Vision
The ultimate dream. Your airlock. Your friend's airlock. Connected. Trading between real players. Visiting each other's bases. Cooperative surface runs. Rival factions competing for the best locations. A monumental engineering challenge — but with the right team and funding, not impossible. This is the horizon. Everything else on this roadmap is the road that leads here.
